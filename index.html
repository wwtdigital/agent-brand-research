Front-End Deliverable

Vertex AI Interface Skin

The clinical AI interface replaces the default Vertex AI Agent Builder chat surface with a fully Ascension-branded experience — so associates interact with a tool that reads as native to Ascension, not a generic AI product.

Brand Identity

Ascension color system applied end-to-end

The skin uses Ascension's exact Pantone-matched tokens: Primary Blue (#1B4297 / Pantone 286) for the header and primary actions, Teal (#009498 / Pantone 321) for accent badges and confirm actions, and Violet (#B40F87 / Pantone 248) reserved for system-level state. Background and border tones are drawn from Ascension's neutral palette, not generic grays.

Clinical Context

Disclaimer and scope notice built into the chrome

A persistent amber disclaimer banner below the header surfaces the agent's clinical scope boundary on every session — no associate can miss it. A dismissible blue config notice can prompt associates to connect the agent to their facility's policy system. Both are structural UI elements, not chat messages, so they cannot be cleared or dismissed by the conversation.

Onboarding

Quick-start cards replace a blank prompt field

The welcome state surfaces four clinical prompt categories as tappable cards — Policy & Procedure, Clinical Quality Data, Medication Safety, and Patient Rights — so associates land on a screen that communicates the agent's capabilities before they type anything. Each card pre-fills a representative query, lowering the barrier to first use.

Attribution

Vertex AI / Google Cloud surfaced, not hidden

The sidebar footer explicitly labels the underlying infrastructure ("Powered by Google Cloud · Vertex AI Gemini") with a chip-style badge. This is intentional: Ascension's AI governance framework requires transparency about AI systems, and the skin reflects that. The branding is Ascension's; the attribution is Google Cloud's.

Analysis

Key Findings

Patterns observed across prompt iterations and scoring runs that shaped the final system prompt design.

Voice

Mission language requires active suppression

Baseline Gemini reliably produced reverence-forward closing lines ("those we are privileged to serve," "the privilege of this ministry") even when unprompted. Explicit hard rules — not soft tone guidance — were required to suppress this pattern consistently across all query types.

Vocabulary

Term substitution is reliable but requires specificity

"Associates" vs. "employees/staff" and "healing without harm" vs. "zero-harm" held consistently once anchored in a required vocabulary table. Partial substitution ("staff members" in place of "staff") required explicit exception patterns in the scorer to avoid false flags on legitimate compound terms like "nursing staff" or "HCAHPS staff responsiveness."

Data Integrity

Metric fabrication is the highest-risk failure mode

Without data guardrails, Gemini produced plausible-sounding CLABSI rates, fall percentages, and HCAHPS deltas — all fabricated. A hard prohibition ("if you do not have a number in front of you, do not write a number") was more effective than softer instructions to "only cite real data." The hard rule eliminated fabricated metrics in the final two runs.

Scope

Scope refusals require declining indicators, not just topic avoidance

Early prompt versions redirected clinical-advice queries without explicitly declining. Scoring showed these as partial failures — the response didn't give harmful advice, but it also didn't model the correct boundary. Adding explicit decline language ("this falls outside my scope") to scope guardrail instructions resolved this.

Tone

Actionability is the most consistently achieved dimension

Every prompt version from Run 1 onward produced actionable closes. The challenge was the form of the action: early runs closed with mission rationale appended after the redirect ("...to support our shared commitment to healing without harm"). Removing trailing sentiment from closes was refined in Run 3 and held in all subsequent runs.

Vocabulary

"Direct leader" vs. "supervisor/manager" is context-sensitive

Ascension uses "direct leader" for reporting relationships but retains formal role titles ("House Supervisor," "Charge Nurse") as proper nouns. The scorer's exception list and the prompt's vocabulary rule both required this distinction to avoid flagging legitimate role references while still catching casual use of "supervisor" or "manager" as generic terms.
